Research & demonstration prototype

Predicting dangerous crowd–traffic interactions before they become operationally critical.

MIIQAT AI reads anonymous movement signals from pilgrims, shuttles, gates and weather, forecasts network risk a short horizon ahead, and tests interventions on a digital twin before a human approves them.

Labels & forecast horizon

A forecasting model can only learn what danger looks like if someone tells it, case by case: “this situation was dangerous.” This page collects those labels and uses them to choose the forecast horizon by experiment instead of by assumption.

Privacy & sensor health

Only aggregate movement signals pass the privacy gate into the network-level risk engine. Sensors can fail; the system keeps running on what remains and says so.

The risk formula

    
Wording we use, and why

      

        
Instead ofWe sayBecause

        
          
“5–10 minute prediction”Short-horizon forecastThe horizon is measured by experiment on labelled data (Labels page), not assumed.

          
“Potential crush risk”Elevated crowd-safety risk · Hazardous crowd stateDescribes the state the system can measure, without predicting a specific disaster.

          
“Identity leakage detected?”Privacy compliance auditAn audit that checks the output against an allow-list, and can be shown to fail.

          
“Open gate / clear lane”Recommended intervention, followed by human approvalThe system suggests; a person decides.

        
      

    

    
Limitations

      
    
        
  • All data is synthetic. Numbers are illustrative and not calibrated to any real site.
    • 
        
  • The risk engine is a transparent weighted formula; it can later be replaced by a trained temporal model.
    • 
        
  • The “dangerous” label used in the horizon test is a synthetic rule (density and speed). Real labels must come from operators and incident reviews.
    • 
        
  • Forecasts and what-if results come from the same simulator; a real deployment needs site calibration and independent validation.
