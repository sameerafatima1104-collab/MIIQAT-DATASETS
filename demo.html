Research & demonstration prototype

Predicting dangerous crowd–traffic interactions before they become operationally critical.

MIIQAT AI reads anonymous crowd readings, forecasts network risk a few seconds ahead, and tests interventions on a digital twin before a human approves them. Here, the crowd side replays real, human-verified HAJJv2 readings, one per second.

What is real and what is not. Zone A replays real readings from two public datasets: HAJJv2-CrowdCount (human-verified crowd counts, one per second, 18 clips) and HAJJv2 (per-frame labels for counter-flow, running, stationary people and moving vehicles). No public dataset has shuttle, road-traffic, emergency-route or heat readings, so those parts are synthetic and marked that way everywhere. The risk score is an interpretable weighted formula, not a trained or validated model, and intervention effects are modelled, not measured.

People detection

A neural network (COCO-SSD with MobileNet v2) runs inside this browser and draws a box around every person and vehicle it finds, frame by frame. Video never leaves the device; only the counts are kept.

Choose a source above. Boxes appear around every person (green) and vehicle (blue) the detector finds.

Privacy mask pixelates the head area of every detected person on screen. Small-person mode splits each frame into four overlapping tiles so distant people are larger; it is slower.

Why the counts differ on HAJJv2 footage. COCO-SSD was trained on everyday scenes, so in dense Hajj crowds it finds only a small share of the people that human annotators counted in HAJJv2-CrowdCount. That gap is why dense-crowd counting models and datasets like HAJJv2 are needed. With a camera pointed at a room or a street, boxes appear around most people.

Labels & forecast horizon

A forecasting model can only learn what danger looks like if someone tells it, case by case: “this situation was dangerous.” This page collects those labels and uses them to choose the forecast horizon by experiment instead of by assumption.

Privacy & sensor health

Only aggregate movement signals pass the privacy gate into the network-level risk engine. Sensors can fail; the system keeps running on what remains and says so.

Limitations

      
    
        
  • Zone A readings are real but short: each HAJJv2 clip is 8 to 26 seconds, so a run lasts that long. Transport, emergency-route and heat parts are synthetic.
    • 
        
  • The risk engine is a transparent weighted formula; it can later be replaced by a trained temporal model.
    • 
        
  • HAJJv2 has no “dangerous” labels. The horizon test therefore measures count-forecast error, not warning time for danger; danger labels must come from operators and incident reviews.
    • 
        
  • Intervention effects are modelled on top of the real readings, not measured. The reference capacity (1,800) is the densest HAJJv2 frame, not a measured capacity for each camera view.
  • The detector is a general-purpose model; it undercounts dense crowds.
